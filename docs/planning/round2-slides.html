One risk definition

A shortfall is an obligation that bank balance plus undrawn drawing power cannot cover. Target: at most 5% chance in 90 days.
Step 1 · When will each buyer pay?

Kaplan-Meier per buyer, because unpaid invoices are information

Share of invoices still unpaid, by days since invoice (from the 24-month ledger)

Why not a plain average? Slow payers are still open. Averaging only paid invoices makes everyone look fast. KM treats an open invoice as "survived this long". Sampling: a 60-day-old CPSE invoice is drawn from the conditional curve, so it is never "paid on day 20". Check: fitted medians match the planted ones within 15% (or 6 days). Planted medians, invoice to payment: Buyer A 64 days, Buyer B 45, CPSE 85, small customers 22. Synthetic data, so this is a self-check, not external validation.
Step 2 · 1,000 simulated futures

One shared shock per buyer: a late buyer is late on every invoice

Bank + undrawn limit, ₹ lakh. Band = P10 to P90 of 1,000 paths. Faint lines = individual paths (with the ₹12 L order)

How a path is drawn: each buyer gets one random shock z_b, each invoice its own z_i. Mix: ρ·z_b + √(1−ρ²)·z_i, with ρ = 0.6. Why it matters: independent draws average out and look safe. Shared shocks keep the bad scenarios. Quarter-end: 30% of paths slip every payment near quarter-end by about a week. Daily loop: receipts, then obligations. Shortfall only counts on days with an obligation due. Orange dashed line = ₹0 of liquidity. ρ = 0.6 and the 0.3 slip are our assumptions, not fitted.
Step 3 · Where the week goes wrong

A late buyer delays the cash and shrinks the limit

Bridge for the worst week (9 Nov), ₹ lakh: start + receipts − obligations ± drawing power = end

Drawing power is the bank's cap on borrowing, recomputed every Monday: min(limit, 0.75 × (stock − unpaid bills) + 0.60 × receivables younger than 90 days). The double hit: as an invoice passes 90 days it stops counting, so the limit falls just when cash is short. Today: bank ₹4.0 L, limit ₹42 L, drawn ₹23.8 L, drawing power about ₹29.1 L. The 25% / 40% margins and the 90-day rule are our assumptions. Check with a banker or CA.

Where the LLM is allowed

Graded plain-language answer, with a digits-only number check

Verdict, graded by the riskiest week's chance of a missed payment

up to 5%YES, safe
5% to 25%YES, BUT TIGHT
25% to 50%RISKY
over 50%NO, NOT AS THINGS STAND

Stops the model from saying "no" to a risk that is only a little over the limit.

Every number in the answer must be a value the engine produced, or one from the owner's own question. A sentence that breaks this is dropped.
CATCHESinvented rupee amounts, wrong days, Devanagari digits handled LIMITdigits only. A number spelled as a word, or a real number used for the wrong thing, can still get through. The prompt and field names guard that. LIMITone sum, the advance in rupees, is done in llm.py, not the engine.

What you should not over-trust

The data is synthetic, and the assumptions are ours

Synthetic

Pune CNC shop, labelled synthetic on every screen. The model is checked against payment behaviour we planted, not against real data. No backtest yet.

Assumptions, not fitted

Buyer correlation 0.6. Quarter-end slip 0.3. Drawing-power margins 25% / 40%. The 90-day rule.Verify with a banker or CA before calling any of it bank practice.

Simplifications

One risk definition for everything. Stock and supplier bills held constant. The new order is invoiced on day 30 and uses Buyer B's curve for its slip.
